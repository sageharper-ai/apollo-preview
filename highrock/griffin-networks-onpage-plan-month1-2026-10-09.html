Overview

Month 1 improves Griffin’s nine main URLs: Home, About, Managed Services, Cabling, Network Solutions, Contact, Partners, Privacy Policy and Insights. HighRock will focus on accurate titles, descriptions, headings, local service copy and site housekeeping so visitors and search engines can understand what Griffin offers and where it works. The intended outcome is a clearer foundation for local search and inquiries; this plan does not promise a ranking or traffic increase.

Before you start

  • Website / CMS access: to record the current settings and implement titles, descriptions, headings, copy and internal links.
  • Hosting access, if possible: to check crawl restrictions, backups, redirects, robots.txt and the sitemap.
  • Google Business Profile access: to confirm ownership and update services, hours, coverage and public contact details.
  • Search Console and analytics access: to check legacy indexed pages and establish a measurement baseline.
What the findings are based on. Automated checks of the home page, robots.txt and sitemap returned HTTP 403 (access denied). The initial findings used public rendered snapshots. A later browser/source check on September 29 recorded HTTP 200 for all nine pages and supplied the titles, descriptions and H1s shown below. Eight pages had no meta description; the home page had no H1. These are September 29 observations, not a new October crawl. The robots.txt and sitemap contents, legacy URL index status, and Yelp/Manta listings still need verification before changes.

Site-wide fixes — in priority order

  1. Local titles, descriptions and headings. Apply the nine recommendations above, starting with Cabling. Add the missing home-page H1 and keep one clear main heading per page.
  2. Google Business Profile and directory cleanup. Confirm the Frederick address and phone, Rockville’s office status, service areas and hours; then complete the profile. HighRock’s strategy identifies old Gaithersburg details on Yelp and Manta, which still need direct verification. The September review also recorded old-address variants on LinkedIn, Help Moving Office and Nextdoor, plus a different Nextdoor phone. Correct records individually after confirming ownership and current details.
  3. Index cleanup. Check the legacy user-access portal and identify the live support destination before redirecting it. Check the legacy managed-solutions URL before consolidating it into Managed Services. Identify and verify the unused template page noted by HighRock before retiring it. Recheck robots.txt, the sitemap, canonicals and index settings with access; the September source check recorded canonicals and index/follow on all nine pages.
  4. Service-area copy and internal links. Put confirmed cities and counties in service-page body copy, not just the footer. Use descriptive links between Cabling, Network Solutions, Managed Services and Contact.
  5. Reviews from existing clients. Griffin leads outreach to clients already quoted on the site, including Cassidy & Associates, Georgetown Day School and Aggregate Management. HighRock supplies a request template so clients can post their own feedback on Google.
  6. Image descriptions and structured data. Review the 42 of 95 images recorded without alt text, including 23 of 27 on Partners; add accurate descriptions to meaningful images and retain empty alt text for decorative ones. The source check found generic site/organization markup but no LocalBusiness, ProfessionalService, Service or address markup. Prepare appropriate local-business and service details after addresses, services and credentials are confirmed.
  7. Verify the finished changes. Recheck source titles, descriptions, H1s, links and mobile layouts after implementation. Record the before-and-after settings and use Search Console and analytics to establish the baseline for later reporting.

Build order for month 1

The working sequence begins when access is available. It follows HighRock’s housekeeping-first, cabling-first direction while staying within the existing-page scope.

  1. Weeks 1–2: access and housekeeping. Confirm business details, preserve current settings, verify the crawl and index questions, and prepare the nine title/description updates. Start Google Business Profile and directory cleanup; prepare Griffin’s review-request template.
  2. Weeks 3–4: cabling first, then the remaining existing pages. Implement the Cabling page’s local copy, headings and related terms first. Follow with Network Solutions, Managed Services and Home, then About, Contact, Partners, Privacy Policy and Insights. Finish internal links, meaningful image descriptions and confirmed structured data; verify what changed.
How this fits HighRock’s larger roadmap. The strategy schedules housekeeping in weeks 1–2; a cabling hub, four location pages and two credential pages in weeks 3–6; trigger pages plus life sciences and private schools in weeks 7–10; and managed IT location pages in weeks 11–16. The new-page program, blog restart and campaign management require separate pricing. This month improves the existing cabling hub and other eight URLs; it does not commit that wider build.

Months 2–3: backlinks, as agreed.
