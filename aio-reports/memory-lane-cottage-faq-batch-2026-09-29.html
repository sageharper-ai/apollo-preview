Q37. When should a dementia patient go into care?

We encourage families to consider whether the person's current support still meets their everyday needs and whether caregivers can sustain that arrangement. There is no single right moment for every family. Discuss safety concerns, increasing assistance needs, and changes in health with the person's physician, then compare care options with those needs in mind.

Site and research draft.

Q38. At what stage of dementia do you need memory care?

We would not use a stage label alone to decide whether a move is appropriate. People's support needs and home circumstances differ, so the decision often involves daily assistance, safety, and the help available to caregivers. Your loved one's physician can advise on medical needs while your family considers suitable living arrangements.

Site and research draft.

Q39. What is the difference between assisted living and memory care?

Assisted living supports everyday needs, while memory care focuses on people living with dementia. At Memory Lane Cottage, we provide memory care only. Our site is fully secured for residents who may wander, with a large secured outdoor area, animals, and certified butterfly gardens. Compare each community's actual staffing, environment, and care, and discuss medical needs with your loved one's physician.

Q40. How do I know when to move from assisted living to memory care?

We suggest starting with a conversation about what the current community can provide and where your loved one may need additional support. A memory care setting may be worth exploring when those needs no longer fit the existing arrangement. Include your loved one where possible, and discuss changes in health or behavior with their physician.

Q41. Is a small memory care cottage better than a large memory care community in Tampa?

We encourage families to look beyond size alone. A smaller setting may suit someone who prefers a more intimate environment, while individual preferences and care needs still matter most. Compare staff interactions, daily routines, shared spaces, and available support. A visit can help you judge how each setting might fit your loved one.

Bring questions specific to your loved one, and take time to notice whether the atmosphere and available support seem appropriate for them.
