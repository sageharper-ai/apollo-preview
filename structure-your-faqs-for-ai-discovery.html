Takeaway #1

What makes an FAQ AI-discoverable

AI doesn't reward more content. It rewards clearer signals. Four rules, in order of impact.

  • Ask the way people ask

    Long-tail, conversational. "How much does a deep-tissue massage cost near me?" beats "Pricing."

  • Answer in 40–80 words

    Long enough to be useful. Short enough to be quotable. One concrete claim per answer.

  • Name a place when it matters

    Local intent? Put your city in the question OR the first sentence of the answer. Not buried at the bottom.

  • Cap each page at ~10 questions

    Beyond that, AI dilutes the page's topic. Better: split into focused topic pages.

Same question. Two answers. Watch what changes.

Q: Do you take walk-ins?

Before

Yes, walk-ins welcome. Please call ahead if possible.

11 words. No location. No concrete time. AI has nothing to quote.

After

Yes — our Charlotte location accepts walk-ins Monday through Friday from 9 AM to 6 PM, and Saturdays until 2 PM. Sundays are appointment-only. To guarantee a time, we recommend booking online the day before.

47 words. Location, hours, concrete claim. AI can lift this verbatim into an answer.

Takeaway #2

FAQPage schema: format, not volume

Schema is a small block of structured data AI search reads first. You don't write it from scratch.

  • One page, one purpose

    A FAQ-rich service page outranks 50 thin blog posts. Every time.

  • Your plugin already does it

    WordPress + Yoast/RankMath/SEOPress, Shopify, Wix — FAQ schema is a toggle.

  • Or paste the prompt

    Free schema-guide prompt at the end of this session generates a starter block.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [{
    "@type": "Question",
    "name": "Do you take walk-ins?",
    "acceptedAnswer": {
      "@type": "Answer",
      "text": "Yes — our Charlotte location..."
    }
  }]
}
</script>

You will never look at this code at 2 AM. Your plugin or the free prompt handles it. The point: AI is reading this format, not your prose.

Takeaway #3

Use AI to find the right questions — not to write 50 blog posts

Wrong

"ChatGPT, write me 50 FAQ articles."

Generic, thin, recognizably AI-generated. Google has been demoting this kind of content for two years. You're now competing with millions of identical pages.

Right

"What are the 15 questions my customers actually ask?"

Use AI to mine — your inbox, your call transcripts, Google's "People Also Ask," your competitors. Then you answer the 15 that matter, in your voice.

AI is your research assistant. You are still the expert.

Three prompts to find your right 15 questions

01

Mine "People Also Ask"

"Search Google for [my main service] in [my city]. Extract every question from the 'People Also Ask' box. Group them by intent: pricing, scheduling, qualifications, what-to-expect."

02

Mine your inbox

"Here are 20 customer emails from the last 6 months [paste]. Pull every question they asked before buying. Cluster them. Tell me which 10 came up more than once."

03

Mine your competitors

"Visit [3 competitor URLs]. List every FAQ they answer. Mark which ones I should answer differently or better. Flag the questions none of them answer that I should own."

Run all three. You'll have your 15 questions in 30 minutes. Then you write the answers.
